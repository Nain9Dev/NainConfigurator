# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Argue about code, not about people.

## Expected behaviour

- Assume competence and good faith in others.
- Give specific, actionable technical feedback. "This breaks company isolation when two requests share a pooled connection" is useful; "this is bad" is not.
- Accept technical criticism of your own work the same way.
- Respect the project's stated boundaries even when you disagree with them. Open a discussion to change a boundary rather than working around it silently.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Personal attacks, insults, or derogatory comments.
- Harassment of any kind, public or private, including unwelcome attention after being asked to stop.
- Discriminatory language or behaviour based on age, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual orientation.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, or repeatedly reopening settled decisions without new evidence.

## Scope

This applies to every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages and code review — and to any situation where someone is representing the project publicly.

## Enforcement

Report a problem privately through GitHub's report abuse flow, or by opening a private security advisory if no other private channel is available.

Reports are handled confidentially. The maintainer will review, decide, and act. Responses range from a private warning, to editing or removing offending content, to a temporary or permanent ban from project spaces. The decision and its reasoning are communicated to the person reported, and the outcome to the reporter.

The maintainer is subject to the same rules as everyone else.
