# Commercial Strategy and Operating Model

Document version: 1.4  
Status: Approved for MVP planning  
Last updated: 2026-07-28

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how NainConfigurator will be sold and operated during the MVP and first paying-customer stage.

Its purpose is to keep the product commercially viable without creating customer-specific code, deployment or data-model branches. It sets the assumptions that user-experience, non-functional, security, architecture and operations documents must implement or measure.

Approval of this document authorizes the next documentation gates. It does not authorize implementation, production use or the processing of real customer data.

## 2. Approved commercial direction

NainConfigurator will begin as a shared multi-company B2B SaaS service for configurable-product businesses. The MVP service combines:

- A one-time paid onboarding and setup engagement.
- A recurring hosted-service subscription.
- Managed catalog and 3D-asset publication by the NainConfigurator team.
- Configurable company identity through data, with no customer-specific code fork.
- Client-owned sales follow-up after a quote request is captured.

The first commercial objective is not to serve every configurable-product industry. It is to prove that the same platform can onboard a paying customer and then a fundamentally different second product without changing the product-specific schema or public contracts.

## 3. Initial ideal customer profile

### 3.1 First target segment

The initial sales segment is small and mid-sized Spanish manufacturers and specialist retailers of configurable furniture, beginning with custom, office and gaming furniture.

The best initial customer:

- Sells made-to-order or configurable products whose options are understandable as a catalog.
- Has one to ten priority product families suitable for visual configuration.
- Uses human commercial confirmation rather than requiring immediate online checkout.
- Needs better product presentation and qualified quote-request capture.
- Does not have an internal team able to build and operate a reusable 3D configurator.
- Can provide accurate product, pricing, compatibility, legal and asset-source information.
- Accepts a managed onboarding process and a shared SaaS release during the MVP.

Enterprise customers that require complex ERP/CPQ integration, contractual 24/7 support, private infrastructure or extensive bespoke workflows are not the first MVP target.

### 3.2 Buyer and users

| Role | Primary need |
|---|---|
| Paying customer | Publish configurable products and receive qualified commercial interest without funding a bespoke platform |
| Customer commercial owner | Trust catalog prices, product rules and incoming quote context |
| Public end user | Understand choices visually, obtain an estimate, save a configuration and request contact |
| NainConfigurator operator | Onboard and support multiple companies through repeatable processes rather than direct production edits |

### 3.3 Expansion rule

Furniture is the first go-to-market segment, not a platform boundary. Expansion to another industry is allowed when the product can use the generic catalog, validation, pricing, snapshot and quote model. A customer request that requires product-specific schema fields or a private code branch must be rejected, generalized as a platform capability or commercially scoped for a later product phase.

## 4. Service and tenancy model

### 4.1 Shared SaaS is the default

The MVP uses one shared multi-company service and one supported application release. Every catalog, configuration, privacy policy and quote request is company-scoped, and cross-company access or data leakage is prohibited.

The approved architecture provides mechanisms that later tests must prove:

- Company context is resolved and authorized server-side.
- Storage queries and background operations preserve company scope.
- Cache keys, logs, metrics, assets and notification routing cannot mix companies.
- One company's traffic or invalid catalog does not corrupt another company's data.
- Deployment and database changes are applied through one controlled product lifecycle.

This document selects the commercial tenancy model; measurable isolation, availability and security requirements are approved in `04.2-NonFunctionalRequirements.md` and `04.3-SecurityAndPrivacy.md`.

### 4.2 Commercial account is not the catalog model

For the MVP, one paying customer is onboarded operationally as one `Company`. However, `Company` remains the public catalog and quote-owning domain concept. It must not be overloaded with future subscription, invoice, user-membership or administrator-permission concerns.

If later customers need multiple brands, legal entities or workspaces under one contract, a separate authenticated commercial-account/control-plane model must be designed. This preserves the current public catalog model and avoids coupling billing structure to product ownership.

### 4.3 Dedicated deployments are deferred

A dedicated deployment is not offered as a standard MVP option. It may become a premium enterprise service only when a paying requirement justifies its additional operational cost.

Any future dedicated service must use the same supported application artifact, contracts and migration path as the shared service. Customer-specific repositories, permanent branches, schema variants and manually maintained application forks are prohibited.

## 5. Revenue and packaging model

### 5.1 Revenue components

| Component | Covers | MVP policy |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding/setup fee | Discovery, catalog normalization, initial asset preparation scope, staging, quality checks, publication and handover | Required and quoted before work begins |
| Recurring subscription | Hosting, supported platform release, agreed capacity, monitoring, backup and standard B2B support | Required while the public service is active |
| Additional professional services | New product onboarding, material asset work, complex data cleanup, approved integrations or exceptional migration work | Separately scoped and priced |

The MVP does not charge per lead, take a commission on the client's sale or use metered usage as the primary invoice model. Operational usage may still be measured to protect capacity, understand cost and define future plan limits.

The approved quality model targets direct recurring infrastructure and managed-service cost at no more than 25 percent of subscription revenue at the planned operating point. Exact prices remain subject to the rate card, but a package that cannot support this cost guardrail is not commercially approved.

Exact prices, included quotas, contract duration, payment terms, taxes and cancellation terms require sales validation and a rate card before the first paid proposal. Changing those commercial numbers must not require changes to the public configurator domain or product catalog schema.

### 5.2 Packaging principles

- The setup fee must cover expected onboarding labor and asset risk; bespoke 3D production is not silently absorbed into the subscription.
- The subscription must have explicit boundaries for active products, asset storage/transfer, traffic and support.
- Extra catalog or asset work is a change request, not unlimited included customization.
- Discounts and pilot pricing are contractual decisions, not product-domain behavior.
- No customer receives a private feature fork in exchange for setup revenue.

## 6. Managed onboarding and catalog operations

### 6.1 MVP onboarding flow

1. **Qualification:** Confirm segment fit, product suitability, expected scope, asset readiness and commercial owner.
2. **Paid scope:** Agree the company, initial products, deliverables, responsibilities, acceptance criteria and excluded work.
3. **Input collection:** Receive structured catalog values, prices, compatibility rules, branding, privacy resource and evidence of asset rights.
4. **Preparation:** Normalize data and prepare or transform agreed visual assets in a controlled workspace.
5. **Staging validation:** Run catalog integrity, visual, pricing, compatibility, accessibility and flow checks outside production.
6. **Customer acceptance:** The customer approves commercial accuracy, presentation and legal resources.
7. **Controlled publication:** Publish a versioned catalog through a repeatable operational mechanism.
8. **Handover:** Confirm lead ownership, support route, update process and go-live responsibilities.

### 6.2 Publication boundary

The MVP does not require a self-service customer administration panel. NainConfigurator operators manage initial publication and approved changes.

Managed operation must not mean untracked production database editing. Architecture and operations must provide a repeatable, validated, auditable and rollback-aware import/publication path. A later administration interface must use the same application validation and publication boundaries rather than introducing a second source of business rules.

### 6.3 Change control

- The customer submits a structured change request.
- Commercial fields and asset changes are reviewed and staged together.
- The customer approves price, descriptions, compatibility and legal content before publication.
- Publication follows catalog-version rules and never mutates saved configuration snapshots.
- Urgent deactivation may stop new commercial activity while preserving approved historical behavior.

## 7. Branding and white-label policy

The MVP supports data-driven company identity without customer-specific UI code. `03.2-UXRequirements.md` approves a versioned `CoBranded` profile with an optional managed logo, validated primary/foreground colors, accessible fallback, company default locale `es-ES` and no arbitrary CSS, executable assets or per-product themes.

The default MVP offer is configurable co-branding. Full white-label may later be sold as a premium capability, but it must remain configuration-driven and use the same release. White-label does not permit removal of legally or operationally required notices, inaccessible color choices, custom commercial logic or a forked client build.

## 8. Responsibility model

| Area | NainConfigurator responsibility | Customer responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Platform | Operate the supported service, releases, technical monitoring, backup and platform security | Use the service within the agreed scope and report issues through the support route |
| Catalog | Normalize, validate and publish through the managed process | Supply and approve accurate products, prices, rules, availability and disclaimers |
| Visual assets | Validate technical format, delivery and renderer compatibility; perform only contracted preparation work | Supply source material, rights and product-accurate visual references |
| Branding | Apply supported configurable identity and validate technical/accessibility constraints | Supply approved brand assets and authorize their use |
| Privacy and legal content | Act as processor for quote data under the DPA; present immutable notice evidence, protect data and execute documented instructions | Act as controller; own the applicable notice, lawful basis, retention justification, rights handling and approval of content |
| Quote intake | Persist safely and, before launch, operate the approved routing/notification mechanism | Designate recipients, monitor leads, respond to users and issue any final commercial offer |
| Support | Provide B2B technical support to the customer contact under the sold service level | Provide product, sales and order support to public end users |
| Go-live | Provide technical readiness evidence | Approve commercial accuracy and accept the launch scope |

The customer is the seller of its configured products. NainConfigurator is not the merchant, does not set the final sale terms and does not promise that an estimated configuration price is a binding offer.

## 9. Lead handling and support model

### 9.1 Quote-request ownership

Each company must designate at least one operational owner for new quote requests before commercial launch. The customer owns response time, qualification, final pricing and sales follow-up.

The current public success response means only that a quote request was persisted. Before a paying launch, later documents must define a secure delivery or work-queue mechanism, failure retries, monitoring, escalation and evidence that the customer can access the request. No email, CRM or notification vendor is selected by this decision.

### 9.2 Support boundary

The standard MVP support model is business-hours B2B technical support for named customer contacts in the initial operating region. It does not include 24/7 support or direct NainConfigurator sales support to the customer's public users.

`04.2-NonFunctionalRequirements.md` defines internal business-hours support, severity, availability and recovery objectives. They remain internal targets until operations are implemented, measured, costed and incorporated into a customer agreement; no general 24/7 human or contractual SLA is currently sold. The narrow Security S1 confidentiality/integrity escalation in `04.3-SecurityAndPrivacy.md` is an internal launch duty, not a general support offer.

## 10. Offboarding and suspension principles

- Non-payment, contract end or customer instruction may deactivate public catalog access through a controlled process.
- Deactivation must not silently delete historical configurations or quote evidence.
- Personal-data retention, deletion, export and backup reconciliation follow the approved `04.3-SecurityAndPrivacy.md` policy.
- Customer asset return or deletion, domain/branding removal and final data access must be covered by the commercial agreement and offboarding runbook.
- Re-activation requires validation that catalog, privacy, routing and contractual information are still current.

## 11. Commercial evidence to collect

The first pilots and paying customers must provide evidence for:

- Sales qualification-to-paid-onboarding conversion.
- Onboarding effort, elapsed time and setup-fee gross margin.
- Recurring revenue, hosting/asset cost and support effort per company.
- Configurator use, saved-configuration and quote-request conversion.
- Customer lead response performance and reported lead quality.
- Catalog-change frequency, asset failure rate and support incidents.
- Retention, expansion to additional products and reasons for churn.

These measures validate pricing and packaging. They must not be used to introduce customer-specific product behavior. Definitions, privacy boundaries and instrumentation will be refined in later requirements and operations documents.
